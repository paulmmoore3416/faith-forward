# Suicide Prevention: Ask, Care, Escort

*By The Reverend Deacon Paul Moore, Founder, Faith Forward Foundation · April 18, 2027 · Week 25*

> **"Why art thou cast down, O my soul? and why art thou disquieted in me? hope thou in God."**
> Psalm 42:11 (KJV)

Too many veterans die by suicide. Every one of them had a name, a story, and people who loved them. You don't need to be a counselor to help. The VA teaches a simple approach called **ACE: Ask, Care, Escort**.

## Ask

If you're worried about someone, **ask directly**: *"Are you thinking about killing yourself?"* Asking does not plant the idea. Research and experience show it often brings relief: someone finally noticed.

Warning signs include talking about death or being a burden, giving away possessions, withdrawing, increased drinking, reckless behavior, sudden calm after deep depression, and getting access to means.

## Care

**Listen without judging.** Don't argue, lecture, or promise secrecy. Say, *"I'm glad you told me. I'm here."* Take it seriously.

If there are firearms or medications around, **ask about putting time and distance between them and the person**: a locked safe, a trusted friend holding them, a gun lock. Reducing access to lethal means saves lives.

## Escort

**Don't leave them alone.** Connect them to help, together:

- Call the **Veterans Crisis Line: 988, press 1**, or text **838255**
- Go to the nearest emergency room
- Call **911** if there's immediate danger

## Faith and the Will to Live

Psalm 42 is a conversation with a downcast soul. The psalmist argues with his own despair: *"Hope thou in God."* If you're the one struggling, borrow that hope today. You are not a burden. You are needed. God isn't finished with your story.

## 🙏 A Prayer for This Week

*God of life, hold back every veteran standing at the edge. Speak to them: stay, you are needed, you are loved. Give us courage to ask the hard question and stay with the answer. Comfort families who have lost someone to suicide. Amen.*

## 🧭 Veteran Resource Spotlight

**Veterans Crisis Line**: **988, then press 1** · Text **838255** · Chat at VeteransCrisisLine.net. Free, confidential, 24/7, for veterans and anyone worried about one.

## 🐕 K9 Corner

For a veteran in a dark season, a dog's needs can be a lifeline: someone depends on them. If you're worried about a veteran with a dog, ask about the dog too. It can open a door to a deeper conversation.
