# Veterans Day and St. Martin of Tours: The Soldier Who Shared His Cloak

*By The Reverend Paul Moore, Founder, Faith Forward Foundation · November 8, 2026 · Week 2*

> **"For I was an hungred, and ye gave me meat: I was thirsty, and ye gave me drink: I was a stranger, and ye took me in: naked, and ye clothed me."**
> Matthew 25:35-36 (KJV)

November 11 is Veterans Day in the United States. It began as Armistice Day, marking the eleventh hour of the eleventh day of the eleventh month of 1918, when the guns fell silent on the Western Front. It is also, by a beautiful coincidence of history, the feast of **St. Martin of Tours**, one of the Church's great soldier-saints.

## The Cloak at Amiens

Martin was a fourth-century Roman cavalry soldier. One bitter winter night at the gates of Amiens he saw a beggar shivering, nearly naked, while crowds walked past. Martin had nothing to give but his military cloak. So he drew his sword, cut the cloak in two, and wrapped half around the freezing man.

That night, tradition says, Martin dreamed of Christ wearing the half-cloak and saying to the angels, "Martin, who is still a catechumen, has clothed me with this garment." Martin was baptized soon after, eventually left the army, and became one of the most beloved bishops in Christian history.

## A Soldier Who Saw

What strikes a veteran about Martin's story is not the sword. It is that **he saw the man**. Everyone else walked by. A soldier, trained to scan his environment and notice what others miss, noticed suffering and acted.

Veterans have that training. We learned situational awareness. We learned to leave no one behind. Faith Forward asks veterans and civilians alike to turn that awareness toward the veteran on the corner, the woman sleeping in her car with her children, the old Vietnam vet at the bus stop with a dog and a bad hip.

## Honoring Veterans Beyond the Parade

Parades and free breakfasts are good. But the best way to honor veterans is to make sure none of them freeze, starve, or die alone. Thousands of veterans across the country are homeless on any given night, and St. Louis has its share. This Veterans Day, we invite you to honor them like Martin did: with half your cloak.

## Our Veterans Day Mission

Faith Forward will hold a prayer service and then take **blessed backpacks** to the street: coats, thermal socks, hats, gloves, hygiene kits, food, and coats and food for dogs. Every item is blessed by a priest before it goes out.

## 🙏 A Prayer for This Week

Like St. Martin, give us eyes to see the suffering veteran and courage to share our cloak. Eternal rest grant to those who gave their lives, O Lord, and let perpetual light shine upon them. Amen.*

## 🧭 Veteran Resource Spotlight

**St. Louis Vet Center** (4735 Fyler Ave, 314-845-8580) offers free, confidential readjustment counseling for combat veterans, service members, and families, often with less wait than traditional clinics. Vet Centers also serve veterans who are not enrolled in VA healthcare.

## 🐕 K9 Corner

Cold-weather tip: short-coated breeds like pit bulls and Malinois lose heat fast. Below 40°F, put a coat on them; below 20°F, limit time outside. For outdoor shelters, use **straw** for bedding, not blankets. Blankets soak up moisture and freeze.

## ✅ Take Action This Week

Donate one new coat or a pack of thermal socks to the Winter Survival Backpack Drive, and thank a veteran today by asking them one question about their service, then listening.
