# Understanding PTSD: What Families and Churches Need to Know

*By The Reverend Deacon Paul Moore, Founder, Faith Forward Foundation · January 10, 2027 · Week 11*

> **"He healeth the broken in heart, and bindeth up their wounds."**
> Psalm 147:3 (KJV)

Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) can develop after a person experiences or witnesses a life-threatening or deeply traumatic event. Many veterans live with it, and so do survivors of assault, accidents, and disasters. PTSD is **not weakness**. It is a recognized, treatable condition.

## Common Signs

PTSD symptoms usually fall into four groups:

1. **Re-experiencing**: nightmares, flashbacks, intrusive memories.
2. **Avoidance**: staying away from people, places, or conversations that bring reminders.
3. **Negative changes in thinking and mood**: guilt, numbness, hopelessness, losing interest in life.
4. **Hyperarousal**: being on edge, startling easily, trouble sleeping, irritability, scanning every room.

## Myths to Retire

- *"All veterans have PTSD."* Many don't. Don't assume.
- *"They should just get over it."* PTSD is a change in how the brain processes threat. Willpower alone won't fix it.
- *"Talking about it makes it worse."* With the right person and the right approach, talking is part of healing.
- *"Faith means you shouldn't need therapy."* God works through doctors and counselors too. Luke was a physician.

## How Families Can Help

- **Learn the triggers** and plan around them together.
- **Don't push for war stories.** Let them share when ready.
- **Keep routines.** Predictability calms a hypervigilant nervous system.
- **Take care of yourself.** Caregiver burnout is real.
- **Know the crisis line**: 988, press 1.

## How Churches Can Help

- Seat veterans where they can see the exits if they want.
- Warn ahead of loud elements (cannons in a hymn, fireworks at a festival).
- Train ushers and greeters in basic veteran awareness.
- Partner with Faith Forward to start a veterans fellowship.

## Treatment Works

Evidence-based therapies such as **Prolonged Exposure** and **Cognitive Processing Therapy** help many veterans reduce symptoms significantly. Faith and community support make the road easier to walk.

## 🧭 Veteran Resource Spotlight

**VA St. Louis PTSD programs** (Jefferson Barracks, 314-652-4100) and the **St. Louis Vet Center** (314-845-8580) offer PTSD assessment and treatment. Coaching Into Care (888-823-7458) helps families encourage a veteran to seek care.

## 🐕 K9 Corner

A trained psychiatric service dog can interrupt anxiety behaviors, wake a veteran from nightmares, and provide deep pressure therapy. Our K9 Suite's Phase 3 teaches these tasks step by step.

Learn the four symptom groups and the crisis line number. Ask your pastor about a veterans ministry.
