# Protection Dogs: Power Under Control

*By The Reverend Deacon Paul Moore, Founder, Faith Forward Foundation · August 22, 2027 · Week 43*

> **"He that is slow to anger is better than the mighty; and he that ruleth his spirit than he that taketh a city."**
> Proverbs 16:32 (KJV)

Many veterans who live outside or in unsafe places want a dog that will protect them. It's understandable. But protection training is serious business, and Faith Forward approaches it honestly.

## What Protection Training Is

Protection training teaches a dog to respond to threats on command and, critically, to **stop on command**. In police and military work and in sports like IGP and PSA, it includes grip development, a reliable release (the "out"), bark-and-hold guarding, handler protection, and, for advanced teams, handler-directed apprehension and **call-off**.

## Control First, Always

The most important protection skills aren't the bite. They're:

- **The out**: releasing instantly on command
- **The call-off**: breaking off pursuit and returning
- **Neutrality**: ignoring friendly strangers

A dog that bites but won't release or recall is a liability, not protection.

## Breed Suitability: Honest Talk

- **Belgian Malinois and working-line German Shepherds** are bred for this work.
- **Labradors** generally lack the drive and are better suited for service and detection.
- **Pit bulls** are often enthusiastic about tug and bite games, but most are bred for low aggression toward people. Many make excellent visible deterrents and handler-protection dogs without full apprehension work.

## Legal and Housing Realities

In Missouri, dog owners face strict liability for bites. A protection-trained dog increases risk and can affect housing and insurance. Protection-trained dogs generally can't function as ADA service dogs. A civilian can only lawfully use force, including a dog, when the law permits self-defense.

## Our Approach

Faith Forward's protection phase is **restricted**: it requires advanced obedience, vet clearance, a professional temperament evaluation, a certified helper, insurance, and Director sign-off.

## 🧭 Veteran Resource Spotlight

Missouri's dog-bite liability statute (RSMo 273.036) and self-defense law (RSMo 563.031) are worth reading. Consult an attorney and your insurer before any protection training.

## 🐕 K9 Corner

Before any protection work, master the two-toy game: play tug, go still, wait for the release, then make the second toy come alive. A rock-solid "out" is lesson one of protection.
