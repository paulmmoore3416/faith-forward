# Moral Injury: The Wound No One Sees

*By The Reverend Paul Moore, Founder, Faith Forward Foundation · December 13, 2026 · Week 7*

> **"Come now, and let us reason together, saith the LORD: though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow."**
> Isaiah 1:18 (KJV)

Many veterans carry a wound that is not exactly PTSD. Clinicians and chaplains call it **moral injury**: the lasting damage that comes from doing, failing to prevent, or witnessing something that violates your deepest sense of right and wrong. It can also come from **betrayal** by leaders or institutions you trusted.

## What It Looks Like

PTSD is often rooted in fear. Moral injury is rooted in **guilt, shame, and anger**. A veteran may say:

- "I can't forgive myself for what happened."
- "God could never forgive me."
- "I don't deserve good things."
- "They used us and threw us away."

Moral injury can drive isolation, substance use, self-sabotage, broken relationships, and spiritual crisis. Veterans may avoid church because they feel too unclean to walk in.

## Why Faith Communities Matter Here

Moral injury is, at its heart, a **spiritual and moral wound**. That is why chaplains, clergy, and faith communities have a unique role alongside clinical care. The Church has two thousand years of experience with guilt, confession, lament, penance, forgiveness, and restoration.

Scripture is full of morally injured people. David, a warrior king, committed adultery and arranged a man's death, then wrote Psalm 51: "Create in me a clean heart, O God." Peter denied Christ three times and was restored three times on a beach. Paul persecuted Christians before he became an apostle. **None of them were beyond grace.**

## Steps Toward Healing

1. **Tell the story** to a safe person: a chaplain, a deacon, a counselor, a battle buddy.
2. **Separate responsibility from guilt.** Some of what you carry is yours; some was never yours to carry.
3. **Lament.** The Psalms give permission to bring anger and grief to God.
4. **Seek forgiveness**, through confession for Catholics and through prayer and community for all.
5. **Make amends where possible**, often through service to others.
6. **Combine spiritual care with clinical care.** Evidence-based therapies help, and faith adds what therapy cannot.

## 🧭 Veteran Resource Spotlight

**VA Chaplain Service**: every VA medical center, including John Cochran and Jefferson Barracks, has chaplains trained in spiritual care for veterans, including moral injury. You can ask for a chaplain during any VA visit.

## 🐕 K9 Corner

Dogs are judgment-free listeners. Many veterans find it easier to say hard things out loud with a hand on their dog. If your dog likes it, practice **"Lap"** (deep pressure therapy): pat your lap, lure their front paws and chest up, and reward calm stillness.

## ✅ Take Action This Week

If you carry a moral injury, tell one safe person this week. If you're a church leader, make it known that veterans with heavy stories are welcome.
