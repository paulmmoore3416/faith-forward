# Fathers Who Came Home Different

*By The Reverend Paul Moore, Founder, Faith Forward Foundation · June 20, 2027 · Week 34*

> **"And he arose, and came to his father. But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran."**
> Luke 15:20 (KJV)

Many veteran fathers came home different. Short tempers. Long silences. Nights out instead of nights home. Some lost their families to the war that followed them home. Many children grew up with a dad who was physically present but far away.

## To Veteran Dads

If you missed birthdays, broke promises, or weren't there, you're not beyond repair. Here's what we've seen help:

1. **Own it without excuses.** "I wasn't there for you, and I'm sorry."
2. **Get help for what's driving it**: PTSD, substance use, anger. Your kids deserve the healthiest version of you.
3. **Show up consistently.** Small, regular presence rebuilds trust better than grand gestures.
4. **Let your kids set the pace.** Some will be ready before others.
5. **Pray for them daily.** Even if they won't talk to you, you can talk to God about them.

## To Children of Veterans

Your dad's struggles are not your fault. It's okay to love him and be angry at the same time. It's okay to set boundaries. Many children of veterans find healing in learning what their parent went through.

## The Father Who Runs

In the parable of the prodigal son, the father **runs** to the son who wasted everything. In that culture, a dignified man never ran. God isn't a dignified distant father; He runs toward us. That love can become a model for veteran fathers, and a healing for every child who missed theirs.

## 🙏 A Prayer for This Week

*Heavenly Father, You run to meet us when we are still far off. Heal veteran fathers and the children who missed them. Give dads courage to say sorry and strength to show up. Give children grace and healthy boundaries. Restore what can be restored, and comfort what cannot. Amen.*

## 🧭 Veteran Resource Spotlight

The **VA** offers family and couples services, and **Vet Centers** provide family counseling for eligible veterans. **Fatherhood programs** in St. Louis help dads reconnect with their children.

## 🐕 K9 Corner

Training a family dog together is a great way for a dad and kids to reconnect: short sessions, shared wins, lots of laughter. Try teaching "Shake" or "Spin" together.

## ✅ Take Action This Week

Veteran dads: take one step this week, like a call, a letter, or an apology. Everyone else: thank a veteran dad.
