# Winter Survival: How to Help a Veteran Living Outside This Season

*By The Reverend Deacon Paul Moore, Founder, Faith Forward Foundation · December 6, 2026 · Week 6*

> **"He giveth snow like wool: he scattereth the hoarfrost like ashes... who can stand before his cold?"**
> Psalm 147:16-17 (KJV)

St. Louis winters are dangerous for anyone living outside. Wind, wet cold, and sudden freezes can turn a hard night into a deadly one. Hypothermia can set in well above freezing when someone is wet, exhausted, or has been drinking. Here is how you can help, practically and safely.

## What Actually Helps

From years on the street, these are the items veterans ask for most in winter:

- **Wool or thermal socks.** Wet feet lead to trench foot, a condition many veterans already know too well. Socks are the number-one request.
- **Hats, gloves, and neck gaiters**
- **Hand and foot warmers**
- **Emergency mylar blankets** and a sturdy, waterproof backpack to keep gear dry
- **High-calorie food**: peanut butter, nuts, jerky, protein bars, pop-top cans
- **Lip balm, petroleum jelly, and hand lotion** for cracked skin
- **For the dog**: a coat, booties, food, and straw for bedding

## Warning Signs of Cold Injury

Know the signs of **hypothermia**: shivering that stops, confusion, slurred speech, clumsiness, extreme drowsiness. Know the signs of **frostbite**: white or grayish-yellow skin, numbness, waxy feel. If you see these, **call 911**. Do not rub frostbitten skin.

## Connect, Do Not Just Give

In St. Louis, the city and partner agencies open warming centers and overflow shelters when temperatures drop. Help the veteran find the nearest one, and ask whether it accepts pets. If it doesn't, call Faith Forward. We will try to find a solution so no one has to choose between warmth and their dog.

## Respect and Safety

Approach with respect. Introduce yourself, offer something tangible, and ask before helping. Never force help on someone. If a person is in medical danger or seems to be in crisis, call 911 or 988 (press 1 for veterans).

## The Blessed Backpack

Every Faith Forward winter backpack is packed with care and **blessed by a priest** before it goes out. That blessing reminds the veteran who receives it that someone prayed for them by name.

## 🙏 A Prayer for This Week

*Lord, it is cold tonight. Cover every veteran sleeping outside with Your protection. Lead them to warmth, keep their feet dry, their minds clear, and their dogs warm beside them. Send us, Lord, with socks and coats and Your love. Let no one freeze alone in the dark. Amen.*

## 🧭 Veteran Resource Spotlight

**The National Call Center for Homeless Veterans**: **877-424-3838**, 24/7. Veterans who are homeless or at risk, and anyone calling on their behalf, are connected to the local VA homeless program.

## 🐕 K9 Corner

**Paw care in winter:** wipe paws after walks to remove de-icing salt, which burns pads and is toxic if licked. Check between toes for ice balls. Booties or paw balm help. Short-coated dogs need a coat below 40°F.

## ✅ Take Action This Week

Pack a winter kit for your car with socks, a hat, gloves, hand warmers, and a protein bar, so you're ready when you see someone in need.
