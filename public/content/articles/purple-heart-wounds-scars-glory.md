# Purple Heart: Wounds, Scars, and Glory

*By The Reverend Paul Moore, Founder, Faith Forward Foundation · August 8, 2027 · Week 41*

> **"Then saith he to Thomas, Reach hither thy finger, and behold my hands... and be not faithless, but believing."**
> John 20:27 (KJV)

August 7 is **Purple Heart Day**. The Purple Heart, descended from George Washington's Badge of Military Merit, is awarded to those wounded or killed by enemy action. It's one of the most respected decorations in the U.S. military, because it's earned in blood.

## Visible and Invisible Wounds

Some wounds are obvious: amputations, burns, scars. Others are hidden: traumatic brain injury, chronic pain, the psychological aftermath of being wounded. Wounded veterans often face years of surgeries, rehabilitation, and adaptation. Many also carry the memory of friends who didn't survive the same blast.

## The Risen Christ Kept His Scars

Here's something remarkable: when Jesus rose from the dead, He **kept His wounds**. He invited Thomas to touch the nail holes and the spear wound. His scars weren't erased; they were glorified. They became the proof of His love.

For wounded veterans, that matters. Your scars aren't something to hide in shame. They tell a story of sacrifice. And in God's economy, wounds can become witnesses of healing and grace.

## Honoring Purple Heart Recipients

- Say thank you, and listen if they want to talk.
- Support adaptive housing, transportation, and employment.
- Make churches and events accessible.
- Remember the families of those who were killed.

## 🙏 A Prayer for This Week

*Wounded Savior, You kept Your scars as signs of love. Bless every veteran wounded in combat. Heal what can be healed, give strength for what remains, and let their wounds become witnesses of courage and grace. Remember those who gave their lives. Amen.*

## 🧭 Veteran Resource Spotlight

The **Military Order of the Purple Heart** supports combat-wounded veterans and their families, including help with VA claims.

## 🐕 K9 Corner

Mobility-limited handlers can still train dogs. Many cues can be taught from a chair, using hand targets, and some service dogs are trained to retrieve dropped items or open doors.

## ✅ Take Action This Week

Thank a Purple Heart recipient, and pray for a wounded veteran by name.
