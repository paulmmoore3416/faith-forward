# The Dog at His Side: Why Animals Matter in Veteran Homelessness

*By The Reverend Paul Moore, Founder, Faith Forward Foundation · November 15, 2026 · Week 3*

> **"A righteous man regardeth the life of his beast."**
> Proverbs 12:10 (KJV)

If you ask an outreach worker what keeps veterans on the street, you'll hear the usual list: addiction, untreated trauma, a criminal record, no ID, broken family ties, a benefits claim stuck somewhere in a stack. Ask a few more questions and you'll hear something else: **"He won't leave the dog."**

## More Than a Pet

For a veteran living outside, a dog does real work:

- **Warmth**: a sleeping dog is a heater on a freezing night.
- **Security**: a dog barks when someone approaches the tent at 3 a.m. For a veteran with hypervigilance, that means sleep.
- **Purpose**: the dog has to eat, so the veteran gets up, walks, and finds food.
- **Love without conditions**: the dog does not care about the discharge status, the arrest record, or the relapse.

Many veterans describe their dog as the reason they are still alive. That is not sentimentality. It is survival.

## The Impossible Choice

Most emergency shelters cannot accept animals, and many transitional and permanent housing programs restrict pets, especially "pit bull type" dogs. So a veteran offered a bed on a cold night faces an impossible choice: the bed, or the dog. Most choose the dog. Then they get labeled "service-resistant."

They are not resistant. They are loyal. They learned loyalty in the military.

## What Faith Forward Does About It

Our K9 program attacks that barrier from several directions:

1. **Healthy dogs**: food, vaccines, flea and heartworm prevention, spay/neuter vouchers, and vet referrals, so a dog meets housing and shelter health requirements.
2. **Well-trained dogs**: foundation obedience, crate training, muzzle conditioning, and Canine Good Citizen preparation, so landlords and shelters see a calm, documented dog.
3. **Assistance-animal pathways**: for veterans with a qualifying disability, we help with emotional-support and psychiatric service task training, and explain their rights under fair housing law.
4. **Advocacy**: our caseworkers ask about pet policies on every housing referral and push for pet-inclusive options.

## Gypse and the First Handler

Our founder, Rev. Paul Moore, is the first handler trained through our program, working with Gypse, a two-year-old blue nose pit bull. He is learning every step so he can train the trainers, so veterans can teach veterans.

## 🧭 Veteran Resource Spotlight

**Humane Society of Missouri** (1201 Macklind Ave, St. Louis) offers veterinary services and low-cost spay/neuter programs. Call ahead to ask about assistance for veterans or people experiencing homelessness.

## 🐕 K9 Corner

**Muzzle training is love, not shame.** A dog that happily wears a basket muzzle can be safely examined, transported, and accepted in more places. Start by smearing peanut butter (xylitol-free!) inside the muzzle and letting your dog put their nose in on their own.
