# Housing First, Faith Always: How Veterans Get Off the Street

*By The Reverend Deacon Paul Moore, Founder, Faith Forward Foundation · April 4, 2027 · Week 23*

> **"Except the LORD build the house, they labour in vain that build it."**
> Psalm 127:1 (KJV)

Across the country, veteran homelessness has dropped significantly since 2010, thanks in large part to targeted programs and a strategy called **Housing First**. It's one of the clearest proofs that ending homelessness is possible.

## What Is Housing First?

Housing First means helping people into permanent housing **without requiring them to "get ready" first** (getting sober, finding a job, or completing treatment). Stability comes first; then services help people keep housing and work on everything else. It turns out it's hard to stay sober or keep appointments while sleeping outside.

## The Key Programs

- **HUD-VASH**: HUD housing vouchers paired with VA case management.
- **SSVF (Supportive Services for Veteran Families)**: rapid re-housing and homelessness prevention, including help with deposits, rent, and utilities.
- **Grant and Per Diem (GPD)**: transitional housing with supportive services.
- **Veterans Community Project, Salvation Army Veterans Residence, MOVE**, and other local programs provide additional options.

## Where Faith Fits

Housing solves homelessness. It doesn't automatically solve **loneliness, purpose, or spiritual hunger**. A veteran alone in a new apartment can feel more isolated than he did in camp with his buddies. That's where the Church comes in:

- Welcome-home kits: dishes, sheets, cleaning supplies, a Bible, and dog bowls
- Regular visits and invitations to fellowship
- Help with budgeting, cooking, and paperwork
- Blessing the new home, a beautiful tradition

## The Pet Barrier

A major obstacle we see is pet restrictions. Faith Forward advocates for pet-inclusive placements and helps veterans document healthy, trained, vaccinated dogs.

## 🐕 K9 Corner

Moving with a dog? Bring a **pet résumé** to landlords: vaccination records, spay/neuter proof, training certificates (like CGC), a reference from a vet or trainer, and a photo of your calm dog. It works.
